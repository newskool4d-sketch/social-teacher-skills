# Evals
This directory contains evaluation rubrics used to benchmark the quality of the K-12 classroom materials generated by the skills published in this repository and similar artifacts. They were developed jointly by **Learning Commons** and **Anthropic** as part of a shared commitment to transparent, field-grounded quality standards for AI in K-12 education.

The goal is AI-generated instructional materials that are explainable and able to be tied to authoritative sources — where every output traces back to validated academic standards, learning science research, and high-quality instructional materials, not an approximation of them.

Each rubric criterion represents a specific aspect of an LLM-generated classroom artifact and is designed to be leveraged in LLM-as-judge prompts, though they can also be applied by human evaluators or adapted for deterministic scoring. We publish them openly so that other developers, researchers, and AI-in-education practitioners can inspect our standards, reuse them, and help improve them. This is a living framework: the criteria here are grounded in extensive literature review and expert input, and we intend to revise them as the field learns more and receives feedback.

## Korean port (2026-08-03) — 1단계: 국가 교육과정 이식

These rubrics are a port of the upstream `k12-teacher-skills` evals to the Korean 2022 revised national curriculum. The P/R/O/M framework, IDs, and the great majority of criteria are unchanged — what changed is every place where a criterion encoded a US-specific assumption and would therefore have scored a correct Korean output as a failure. This stage was scored against science lessons; the subject move to 사회 is the second stage, recorded below.

| Upstream assumption | Replaced with | Criteria |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| NGSS three dimensions (SEP / DCI / CCC) | 2022 개정 내용 체계 3범주 (지식·이해 / 과정·기능 / 가치·태도) | `P-C1`, `P-C8`, `P10` (differentiation) |
| Crosscutting concept named and applied | 가치·태도 stated observably and actually observed | `P-C8` |
| CER writing at every grade | 주장-근거, gated by 학년군 | `R-C1` |
| Model revision in every lesson | Branches on the standard's verb (과학판: 실험 설계형 → 설계 검토) | `P-C7` |
| State / state-standards question | 학년·학교급 question, plus the 초1–2 no-subject case | `M-CLARIFY-GRADE`, `M-CLARIFY-G12` |
| Standard text always verbatim | 중등 verbatim; 초등 conditional — see the 2026-08-30 revision below | `P1`, `M1` |
| Prerequisite always sourceable | Learning-map edge preferred; explicitly marked 추정 accepted when the map has none | `P2` (both rubrics) |
| At least 3 misconceptions | 2–3, since the subject reference caps the section at 3 | `P4a` |
| IM / OpenSciEd curriculum bleed-through | 검정 textbook publisher neutrality | `M5`, `P9` |
| Below / At / Above tiers, Groups A–C | 가 / 나 / 다 모둠 (기초 / 보통 / 심화) | `O1`, `P4`, `R2`, `O5` |
| WIDA English Language Development | 다문화·한국어 지원, 개별화교육계획 | `M4` |
| Math/ELA examples (number line, tape diagram, story map) | Subject-appropriate representations | `P6` |

Each ported criterion's `Notes` column records what it replaced and why, so the diff against upstream stays auditable. Upstream's math and ELA rubrics are not included.

Two companion documents, both generated by [`docs/evals_port_review.py`](../docs/evals_port_review.py), make the port auditable from both sides: [`docs/evals-port-review.html`](../docs/evals-port-review.html) puts every changed criterion beside its upstream original, and [`docs/evals-unchanged-criteria.md`](../docs/evals-unchanged-criteria.md) prints the 38 criteria carried over untouched. The second exists so "we only changed what was broken" is something a reader can check rather than take on trust.

The review page sorts each change into one of three kinds, and the third is the one to scrutinise:

- **측정 대상 부재 (12)** — the construct does not exist in the Korean system or the learning-map data, so the criterion's verdict was constant regardless of lesson quality. Fixing an unmeasurable criterion, not lowering a bar.
- **계측기 수리 (14)** — the judge was matching on strings the skill never renders (`Below tier` vs `가 모둠`). Strictness unchanged.
- **우리 스펙 편 (6)** — the upstream criterion conflicted with this project's own `science.md` and we resolved it our way. **This is the category a reader should audit**, because the justification comes from an artifact we wrote ourselves. It is flagged in the review page rather than buried.

Two of the changes were *stricter* than upstream, not looser: `P1` failed a lesson that claimed 초등 text was verbatim, and `P4a` fails a lesson carrying more than three anticipated-thinking entries. Neither failure mode existed before. (The `P1` direction was reversed by measurement in the 2026-08-30 stage below — 초등 원문은 실제로 공식 원문이었다.)

Three shared criteria moved their bar rather than their wording, and each one moved **stricter**:

- **`P1` (초등 분기)** — the 2026-08-03 port assumed elementary standard text was a mechanical derivative and demanded a "재구성" footer. Measurement (`docs/superpowers/social-map-survey.md` §3-2) found the opposite: since map v0.5.0 the elementary `officialText` is the official NCIC text, and the record's `sourceTextIncluded: false` is a stale flag. P1 now requires 초등 text verbatim like 중등, fails a lesson that calls it a reconstruction, and keeps a footer requirement only for the ~8% of records carrying a visible extraction defect. Verbatim is judged as byte identity with the map's `officialText` — the word-spacing artifacts the official extraction left in it (`해결 하기`, `이해 한다`) must be preserved, not normalised.
- **`P2` (both rubrics)** — social standards have almost no citable prerequisite edge (one across 252 secondary topics), and elementary edges join sub-topics inside the same standard and are workstream-authored. 추정 marking is therefore the default path, and citing an elementary edge as official grounding is now a fail.
- **`P2` 심화 branch (differentiation)** — social carries zero transition records, and the skill forbids "이후 ___로 심화된다". The criterion no longer asks for a forward standard; it grounds 심화 in R7's validity conditions and fails a transition claim.

이와 별개로 shared 두 항목 — `P6b`(look-for의 범주 귀속)와 `O3`(관찰 템플릿의 현장 사용성) — 은 **문면만
고쳤고 산출물 판정은 바뀌지 않았다.** 두 건은 **바를 옮긴 것이 아니라 계측기를 고친 것**이다(2026-08-30 fix 회차). `P6b`는 pass 절의 검증
불가능한 요구("왜 중요한지")를 판정 가능한 대체물(관찰 템플릿이 범주 접두의 용도를 한 줄로 밝히는가)로
바꿔 Fail 절과 정합시켰고 — 실채점이 이미 Fail 절 기준으로 이뤄졌으므로 이 방향이 판정 보존이다 —
교사 동작의 구체성을 fail 조건으로 새로 명시했다. `O3`은 학생 행동이 **표의 행 라벨**로 오는 배치만
인정하던 문면을, 이 스킬이 실제로 렌더하는 배치('관찰 포인트 목록 + 그 아래 기록 격자')에서도 판정할 수
있게 다시 썼다(2026-08-06 과학판에서 판정 편차로 기록된 자리다). 배치 자유도는 넓어졌고 fail 조건은
유지된다.

`docs/evals-port-review.html` and `docs/evals-unchanged-criteria.md` document the 2026-08-03 upstream→Korean stage only; they are kept as the science-stage lineage record and do not cover this second stage.

## First run (2026-08-06) — 과학판 기록

이 절은 1단계(과학) 실채점의 보존 기록이다. 대상 패키지·루브릭이 모두 과학판이므로 사회과
루브릭의 현재 수치가 아니다 — 현행 수치는 아래 2026-08-30 절을 본다. `science.csv`가 삭제되어
`runs/2026-08-06/`의 판정 파일은 더 이상 `aggregate.py`로 재집계되지 않는다(선언된 루브릭
경로가 사라졌다). 그 실행의 `summary.md`는 이미 기록으로 남아 있다.

`runs/2026-08-06/` holds the scores: four lesson packages
(초4 [4과10-02]; 중2 [9과12-01] ×3) × all 40 planning criteria = 160 cells, one LLM judge per
package with the learning-map MCP called live to verify P1 (official text) and P2 (prerequisite
edges) against ground truth rather than against the document's own claims.

**149 pass / 6 fail / 5 skip — 96.1% of scored cells.** Five of the six fails were re-verified
against the source documents by a second pass and confirmed as real defects; the sixth (`O3` on
one package) was a judge-variance artifact — the same observation-grid layout passed in the other
three packages, so the criterion, not the package, is what needs work. Per-criterion detail,
including every explanation, is in [`runs/2026-08-06/`](runs/2026-08-06/).

What this does and does not establish: the port's criteria are no longer only calibrated against
the skill's spec — they have now been calibrated against observed judge behaviour once, on a
science-only, 4-package, single-judge sample with no 고교 lesson in it. `M3` could not be scored
at all (it judges the chat response, and these are archived artifacts), and `R-S2` is skipped for
초등 by its own conditional. Treat the pass rate as a first reading, not a benchmark.

## How to use these rubrics

Each rubric is a CSV with the following fields:

| Field | Description |
| ----- | ----- |
| `ID` | Unique criterion identifier (e.g., `P1`, `R3`) |
| `Bucket` | Top-level category: `P` (Pedagogy), `R` (Rigor), `O` (Output/Formatting), or `M` (Model Scaffolding) |
| `Criterion` | Short name for the criterion |
| `What pass requires` | The specific, scoreable condition that constitutes a pass |
| `Notes` | Rationale or design notes |
| `Conditional` | If non-empty, the criterion applies only when this condition is met (e.g., `중·고-정량자료`, `초1-2`, `grade-unknown`) |

For lesson plan generation, apply `shared.csv` first, then layer in `social.csv`. Subject-specific criteria extend the shared set.

Conditional criteria (marked in the `Conditional` column) apply only when the specified condition is met — for example, a criterion that applies only to 통합사회 lessons, or only to a lesson that hands students two sources. If the condition isn't met, the criterion is skipped (not failed).

Criteria score independently — a failing `R2` tells you something specific about cognitive demand, not just that the output is "bad." Depending on your situation, consider tracking per-criterion pass rates across a prompt suite rather than relying on aggregate scores, since aggregate pass rates can mask meaningful gaps.

### Running as LLM judge

You'll have to do some manual setup to use the rubrics, or you can feed them into an existing eval harness you have running.

1. Start with a set of lesson materials (you can utilize the skills in this repo to generate a new set)  
2. Utilize an LLM and pass the lesson materials and associated rubric CSVs along with an LLM as judge prompt to instruct the model to score the lesson materials as either `0` or `1` against each rubric criterion

You can utilize this system prompt to setup your LLM as judge:

```
You are a rigorous educational content evaluator. Your job is to assess whether
AI-generated lesson plan documents meet specific rubric criteria.

You will receive:
  1. The lesson-plan documents as attached files.
  2. The model's final chat response (the text it sent back to the user).
  3. A rubric with criteria to judge against.

Grading rules:
  - Judge criteria in the `M` (Model Scaffolding) bucket against the chat
    response (M2는 문서 판정 예외). Judge all other criteria against the attached
    documents — the content must actually be present in the documents, not
    merely claimed in the chat response.
  - Pass means the criterion is clearly and fully met. Fail means it is absent,
    incomplete, or only partially met.

Respond ONLY with a valid JSON array — no preamble, no markdown fences, no
trailing text. Each element: {"id": "...", "pass": true|false,
"explanation": "one sentence"}.
```

You may also want to create individual LLM judges with prompts that provide more detailed instructions for each rubric criterion. 

Over time, an important aspect of using these criteria effectively is calibrating the judging by modifying the `What pass requires`  or tuning the prompt for your LLM judges. We suggest a simple loop of partnering with education practitioners or researchers.

## The P/R/O/M framework

All criteria fall into one of four buckets. The buckets reflect two paired goals: **quality** (does the output reflect strong pedagogy and appropriate rigor?) and **usability** (is the output formatted and scaffolded in a way that a real teacher can actually use it?).

### P — Pedagogy

Pedagogy criteria evaluate whether the output reflects sound instructional design: standards alignment, prerequisite and forward connections, appropriate instructional model, discourse structures, attention to student struggle, and visual/representational choices. These are the criteria most directly grounded in curriculum research and learning science.

Key pedagogical commitments threaded through the P criteria:

* **Textbook neutrality.** Korea runs a single national curriculum with 검정 (state-authorized) textbooks from multiple publishers. Outputs align to the 성취기준 itself and never reproduce a publisher's activities, readings, illustrations, or items — replacing upstream's HQIM-coherence commitment, which assumed adopted-curriculum variation that does not exist here.  
* **Discipline-specific instructional models.** The 사회 rubrics require source-based inquiry, evidence-anchored argument writing, and controversy handled controversially.  
* **Anticipating struggle.** Rather than only naming "misconceptions" (which implies incorrect beliefs), P criteria require attention to *points of difficulty* more broadly: patterns, why they arise, and teacher moves for each. Generic "some students may struggle" language fails.

### R — Rigor

Rigor criteria evaluate whether the output maintains grade-level cognitive demand. R criteria are designed to catch any downward drift in the intellectual work students are asked to do.

Three specific checks:

* **Grade-level access for all students.** No materials simplify below the standard's cognitive demand without a scaffold that preserves the core challenge. Access supports are additive, not reductive. This applies across tiers in differentiated materials — below-level materials include the full standard, including its hardest cases, with scaffolding, not a simplified version of the task.  
* **Critical thinking demanded.** At least one task or prompt must require students to explain reasoning, evaluate a strategy, or connect representations, not just produce a correct answer.  
* **Student agency.** Student-facing materials must include at least one open-ended or reflective prompt, not only guided or structured practice.

### O — Output/Formatting

Output criteria evaluate the artifact itself: correct file structure, appropriate length, teacher- vs. student-facing separation, universal design features, and teacher rationale notes. These exist because a pedagogically strong lesson plan that is too long, confusingly formatted, or missing student materials has limited real-world utility.

One criterion worth calling out: **designed for teacher adaptation.** Teacher-facing outputs should explicitly note which elements are non-negotiable (curriculum sequence, grade-level demand, discourse structure) and why — so teachers who want to adapt have a principled basis for knowing what to preserve.

### M — Model Scaffolding

Model Scaffolding criteria evaluate the model's conversational behavior, not the artifact content: whether it asks for missing information before generating, whether it proactively produces student-facing materials without re-prompting, and whether it offers meaningful follow-up options at the close of Turn 1\.

## **Data dependency**

In this port the Learning Commons Knowledge Graph is replaced by the two Korean curriculum learning-map MCP servers (`curriculum-kr-secondary`, `curriculum-kr-elementary`), which supply 성취기준 official text, prerequisite edges, and transitions. Criteria that reference map-sourced content — P1 (official text), P2 (prerequisites), P-C7 (standard-type branch) — need those servers connected to score accurately against real outputs, or a ground-truth transcript of the same records (this is what `pilot/*/ground-truth.md` supplies).

Two data limits are baked into the criteria rather than assumed away. Elementary records still carry the stale `sourceTextIncluded: false` flag while the served `officialText` is the official NCIC text, so P1 requires it verbatim and asks for a check-the-고시문 footer only where the extraction is visibly defective. Social standards return `directEdges: []` almost everywhere and zero transitions, so P2 makes an explicitly marked 추정 the default path and treats an official-sounding edge citation as a fail.
